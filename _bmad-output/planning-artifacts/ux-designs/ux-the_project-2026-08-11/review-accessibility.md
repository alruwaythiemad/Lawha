# Accessibility Review — the_project (Lawha)

*Reviewed: DESIGN.md, EXPERIENCE.md, .memlog.md, .working/direction-signal.html. Every contrast figure below was computed from WCAG 2.x relative luminance (sRGB linearisation, `(L1+0.05)/(L2+0.05)`), not sampled from a tool.*

## Overall verdict

**Not shippable as written against WCAG 2.2 AA — but the failures are concentrated, not systemic.** The palette arithmetic is genuinely sound (seven of nine stated ratios are exact to the published decimal, and the two misses are conservative), and the spine's most-questioned premise — "health is the absence of signal" — survives 1.4.1 scrutiny because every status tag carries a text label. What sinks it is that the product's single most important object, the non-dismissible alarm banner, has **no conformant treatment for interactive content or focus on a `#FF2D00` ground** (worst case 1.03:1 in dark mode), the entry point is an explicitly-unaudited third-party surface whose audit is deferred *past* the Arabic launch, and owner-uploaded video plays full-screen on a public wall with no flash-safety gate at all (2.3.1, Level A).

Roughly two-thirds of the findings below are **specification gaps rather than design dead-ends** — the spine repeatedly states an accessible *outcome* ("tab order equals visual order", "targets ≥44px", "partial is stated") without specifying the mechanism that would make it true or the thing that must be forbidden to keep it true. Those are cheap to close. The three Critical items are not.

## Findings

### Critical (3)

- **C-1 — The alarm banner is the product's only alerting channel and the least accessible object in the system.** (`DESIGN.md` › `components.banner-alarm`, `components.focus-ring`; `EXPERIENCE.md` › *Component Patterns* › Alarm banner.) The banner is a solid `#FF2D00` fill that per EXPERIENCE.md "links to the disclosure rather than restating it" — so it contains interactive content — and per the Truth Contract it is the **only** channel through which an owner learns a screen went dark. Every available treatment fails:
  - The link in `{colors.electric}` (the spine's "only interactive colour") is **2.003:1** on `#FF2D00` → fails 1.4.3 (needs 4.5:1) and 1.4.11.
  - The link in `{colors.alarm-foreground}` `#000000` passes contrast at 5.627:1 but is then **visually identical to the surrounding banner text** — no underline (the spine boasts electric "lets a link be a link without underline gymnastics"), no colour, no weight rule → fails **1.4.1 Use of Color** by having *no* visual carrier at all, which is worse than the failure mode 1.4.1 was written for.
  - The focus ring on that link is `#0F2BFF` at **2.003:1** in light mode and `#5C7CFF` at **1.028:1** in dark mode against the banner it sits on. At 1.03:1 the ring is not merely non-conformant, it is *not rendered to the human eye*. Fails 1.4.11 (3:1 for component states) and, in practice, **2.4.7 Focus Visible (Level A)**. This is caused by the spine's own rule that "alarm and amber do not change between modes" colliding with electric being the only token that does.
  - Same arithmetic condemns any focus ring adjacent to amber in dark mode (**2.094:1**), and the 2px `outlineOffset` means a ring on an element merely *abutting* the banner bleeds onto red — guaranteed, because `{spacing.section-gap}` is 0.
  - The rendered reference direction, `.working/direction-signal.html`, sets `.strip{color:#FFF}` and `.strip a{color:#FFF;text-decoration:underline}` — **white on `#FF2D00` at 3.732:1**, the exact combination DESIGN.md names as failing. The spine corrected the mock but nothing in the mock was re-rendered, and `mockups/` (cited by both spines as holding the promoted key screens) does not exist, so the failing artefact is the only visual reference an implementer has.

  *Fix:* add a third signal-surface foreground pair — a `focus-ring-on-signal` token of `#000000` (21:1 on nothing, but **5.627:1** against alarm and **12.108:1** against amber) and mandate a double ring (2px black outer + 2px white inner, or `outline` plus `box-shadow`-free `outline` on a 2px white `outline-offset` gap) for any focusable element on or adjacent to a signal fill. Separately, require that links on signal fills are underlined — this is the one place the system must accept underline, because it has spent every other differentiator.

- **C-2 — The AA commitment is made on a surface the spine explicitly declines to specify, and the audit is deferred past the Arabic launch.** (`EXPERIENCE.md` › *Bilingual Behaviour* › Open risk; *Information Architecture* › Sign up / sign in; Flow 1 step 1.) Clerk hosted auth is step 1 of the only validated journey, is mandatory for 100% of users, and the spine's position is *"this spine cannot specify the sign-up surface's mirrored behaviour, because it is not ours to specify"* with a revisit gate of *"before phase 3 Arabic work begins, not during it."* Accessibility conformance is not delegable: under EN 301 549 / the EAA the service provider answers for the whole service including embedded third-party components, and a WCAG 2.2 AA claim covers **complete processes** (Conformance Requirement 3 — if any page in a process fails, the process cannot be claimed conformant). Unverified at the front door, all in one place: 3.1.1/3.1.2 (`lang`/`dir` on the hosted page — if Clerk does not emit `lang="ar" dir="rtl"`, an Arabic screen-reader user gets Arabic text spoken by a Latin voice engine, which is a total failure, not a degradation), 1.4.3 (Clerk's default tinted greys, which this palette rejects on principle), 2.4.7/1.4.11 (Clerk's focus ring, which will not be the never-suppressed 2px electric ring the spine mandates), 3.3.8 (whether Clerk's email-code flow permits paste), 1.4.10 at 320px, and 2.5.8. The stated deferral means Arabic ships non-conformant at its entry point *by plan*.
  *Fix:* move the Clerk decision from "revisit before phase 3" to a **launch gate**: run an EN 301 549 smoke test (NVDA/VoiceOver + `ar` locale + 400% zoom + keyboard-only) against Clerk's hosted pages *now*, before the Arabic phase is scoped, since the outcome determines whether headless components are a phase-3 task or a phase-1 architectural commitment. Until it passes, the spine's Accessibility Floor must scope its claim explicitly ("AA within `apps/console` and `apps/player`; authentication conformance unverified") rather than stating an unqualified product-level AA target.

- **C-3 — Owner-uploaded video plays full-screen on a public wall with no flash-safety gate. 2.3.1 is Level A and is entirely unaddressed.** (`EXPERIENCE.md` › *Component Patterns* › Media uploader; *Responsive & Platform* › Player.) The uploader validates exactly three things — format, size (150 MB video / 15 MB image), and codec (FR-25, FR-26). **2.3.1 Three Flashes or Below Threshold (Level A)** applies to all content the service delivers, including user-generated content, and the player is a 1920×1080 full-bleed surface at 2–4 metres in a public venue. A café owner uploading a supplier's promo reel with a strobe cut can trigger a photosensitive seizure in a passer-by who never chose to look at this screen and has no way to stop, pause, or leave the content's field of view. This is the one finding here with a physical-harm and EU product-liability dimension rather than a usability one, and neither spine, the memlog, nor the direction mentions flashing, strobing, or seizure safety anywhere.
  *Fix:* add flash-safety to the upload contract as a first-class ceiling alongside size and codec — at minimum a stated policy plus a warning-and-acknowledgement at upload for video, and ideally server-side luminance-flash analysis (PEAT/Harding-style) on ingest, with rejection copy in the same shape as the existing codec rejection ("names the specific reason and the fix"). Also state a plain-language content policy the owner sees at the point of upload, since he is the one publishing to the public.

### High (6)

- **H-1 — 4.1.3 Status Messages: the product has one live region and needs at least seven.** (`EXPERIENCE.md` › *Accessibility Floor*, bullet 6.) The spine specifies exactly one `aria-live="polite"` region, for a screen going offline. Every other dynamic status in the product is silent to assistive technology: per-file upload progress and per-file rejection where "a failed file does not fail the batch"; the optimistic-then-rolled-back rename/reorder/duration/branch-move ("apply immediately with rollback on failure" — the rollback is a status change the user did not initiate); "the editor has no Publish button — it states when the change reached each assigned screen instead" (a *continuously arriving* status, the flagship FR-37 moment); at-limit and storage-exhausted blocks; the schedule editor's "which window wins and why" sentence "at the moment of editing"; the branch bulk-assignment count-and-overrides confirmation and its undo; and the partial-load statement. Each of these is a 4.1.3 (AA) failure for a screen-reader user, and H-2 makes the set worse.
  *Fix:* specify live-region policy at the pattern level rather than per-instance — a single global `role="status"` announcer service with a documented politeness/`aria-atomic` contract, plus a rule in *State Patterns* that every state in the empty/loading/partial/error/stale/at-limit/stopped matrix must name its announcement channel. Reserve `role="alert"` for the rollback case only (an unrequested reversal of the user's action is genuinely interruption-worthy, unlike a screen going offline).

- **H-2 — "No signal" is indistinguishable from "not yet loaded" for a screen-reader user, and the spine removed the only cue that would have separated them.** (`EXPERIENCE.md` › *State Patterns* › "Loading never shifts layout"; *The Truth Contract* › "Calm until it matters".) Sighted users are fine — a skeleton row is textless. But the loading state is defined as "a divider-separated block of the correct height — **nothing animates** (there is no shimmer; the system has no gradients)", and no `aria-busy`, `role="status"`, or "Loading screens" announcement is specified anywhere. So for an AT user the three quiet states — *loaded and healthy*, *still loading*, and *loaded but partial* — collapse into the same silence, on a product whose entire thesis is that silence means "verified healthy". This is the specific failure mode the audit brief asked about, and it is real: the premise "health is the absence of signal" is only safe if "absence" is *reliably* distinguishable from "absence of data", and here it is not. 1.3.1 and 4.1.3. (Note: "Partial is stated, not hidden" is exactly the right rule and is the strongest state-pattern in the spine — it just has no programmatic mechanism.)
  *Fix:* `aria-busy="true"` on the region while loading plus a polite "Loading screens" / "Screens loaded, N of N reporting" pair; and make the healthy state *positively* announced rather than inferred — the row's accessible name should end with "Live, confirmed just now", which costs nothing visually and converts an absence into an assertion. Visual health can stay silent; the accessibility tree must not.

- **H-3 — The pairing code is transcription-only with paste explicitly forbidden, an unspecified character set, and type sized to break narrow reflow.** (`EXPERIENCE.md` › *Component Patterns* › Pairing code input, *Interaction Primitives* › Copy affordances; `DESIGN.md` › *Typography*, `typography.code`.) Three compounding problems:
  1. **3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (Minimum), AA.** Device pairing authenticates a player into the workspace. The spine states *"The pairing code is not copyable — it is read off a TV, in the other direction"* — so the transcription cannot be avoided by paste, autofill, or a password manager, and no alternative path exists (it is "the only path a screen enters the workspace"). The usual escape hatch for one-time codes is precisely that they can be pasted; that hatch is closed here **by design decision**. For a user with a working-memory impairment, dyslexia, or low vision, a six-character alphanumeric read at 2–4 metres and retyped on a phone is the hardest single act in the product.
  2. **The alphabet is never specified.** The mock shows `7K2QF9`. Nothing anywhere excludes `0/O`, `1/I/l`, `5/S`, `2/Z`, or `8/B`, and `{typography.code}` is Inter 800 — a grotesk whose zero is undotted by default. The spine chose 56px + 0.1em tracking for cross-room legibility and then left the character set, the actual determinant of ambiguity, undefined.
  3. **1.4.10 Reflow.** `{typography.code}` is 56px at 0.1em tracking and is not scoped to the player. Six glyphs plus tracking is roughly 370–390px of inline size before padding; the spine's own narrow constraint is 390px ("Pairing must work at 390px in particular") and 1.4.10 requires 320px. If the console input inherits `typography.code`, it overflows both.

  *Fix:* restrict the code alphabet to a Crockford-style unambiguous set (drop `0 O 1 I L 5 S 2 Z 8 B`) and say so in DESIGN.md next to `typography.code`; scope `typography.code` to the player and give the console input its own smaller tier; and add a non-transcription alternative for 3.3.8 — the player already renders a code, so having it also render a QR that deep-links the console to a pre-filled pairing URL removes the cognitive-function test entirely while *improving* Yusuf's fifteen-minute path.

- **H-4 — The screen row is a single link with no visual interactive affordance and an accessible name that collides with the no-concatenation rule.** (`EXPERIENCE.md` › *Component Patterns* › Screen row; *Voice and Tone* › Mechanically (AD-22).) "Rows are not selectable; the whole row is one link to Screen detail with a single accessible name." Two consequences the spine never resolves:
  - **Nothing marks the row as interactive.** Row text is `{colors.foreground}` black and `{colors.muted-foreground}` grey, not electric; there is no underline; radius, shadow and *transition* are all zero so there is no hover or press affordance in the system to fall back on; and the only boundary between one 48px link and the next is a `#DADADA` divider at **1.398:1**. DESIGN.md's own rule — divider "is decorative, **never the only thing distinguishing two interactive targets**" — is violated by the console's primary unit, on the home surface. This escapes 1.4.1 on a technicality (no colour is used, so colour is not the *sole* carrier) while failing its intent, and fails 1.4.11 to the extent the row boundary is required to identify the component.
  - **The accessible name is an assembled sentence.** Four columns (name, playlist, last-confirmed time, status tag) folded into "a single accessible name" is by definition concatenation — which AD-22 forbids: "No concatenation, no template interpolation, no sentence assembly in application code." One of the two rules has to bend, and the spine does not say which.

  *Fix:* give the row a stated non-colour affordance that survives zero-motion (a `cursor: pointer` plus a full-strength `{colors.border}` divider promotion on hover/focus-within is inversion-consistent and free), and specify the row's accessible name as **one ICU catalogue entry with four named slots**, so AD-22 and the single-name rule agree. State explicitly that the status tag is non-interactive (see M-2).

- **H-5 — No bypass mechanism, on a product where two persistent blocks precede every page's content. 2.4.1, Level A.** (`EXPERIENCE.md` › *Component Patterns* › Alarm banner, Navigation; Flow 2 step 5.) A keyboard user meets, on every page load, a persistent 186px nav rail (six-plus items) and — whenever any screen is offline, which the spine treats as the common case — a full-bleed non-dismissible banner containing a link, positioned as "the first thing on the page". No skip link, no landmark structure, and no `role="navigation"`/`role="main"`/`role="banner"` assignment is specified anywhere in either spine. 2.4.1 Bypass Blocks is Level A. (Note: **2.2.4 Interruptions is AAA**, so non-dismissibility is *not* an AA failure and the product-owner's accepted risk stands — the AA problem is bypass and landmarks, not persistence.)
  *Fix:* specify a skip-to-content link (which the zero-radius, zero-motion system can render as a plain black-on-white block on focus) and a landmark map in the Accessibility Floor. Since the banner is "the only channel", also give it `role="region"` with an accessible name so a screen-reader user can navigate *to* it deliberately rather than only past it.

- **H-6 — Twelve component specs reference light-mode tokens only, and no theme-swap rule exists. Dark-mode contrast is asserted, not derivable.** (`DESIGN.md` frontmatter › `components.*`.) `status-tag-live.border` is `{colors.divider}`, `status-tag-live.foreground` is `{colors.foreground}`, `table-row.borderBlockEnd` is `{colors.divider}`, `button-secondary.foreground` is `{colors.foreground}`, `focus-ring.outline` is `{colors.electric}`, `meter.fill` is `{colors.foreground}`, `nav-item-active.background` is `{colors.foreground}`, and so on — all light-mode literals. The `-dark` variants exist in the colour block but nothing states the substitution rule, so "both themes" is unverifiable from the artefact and an implementer reading the frontmatter literally ships `#000000` text on `#0A0A0A`. The one place the spine *does* reason about a dark substitution (electric → electric-dark) proves the swap is not mechanical: alarm and amber deliberately do **not** swap, which is exactly what produces C-1.
  *Fix:* state the rule once ("every `{colors.X}` reference in `components.*` resolves to `{colors.X-dark}` under `[data-theme=dark]`, except `alarm`, `amber`, and their `-foreground` pairs, which are theme-invariant") and then enumerate the exceptions' consequences — which will surface C-1 automatically.

### Medium (7)

- **M-1 — The `#DADADA` divider at 1.398:1, and the "Live" tag border question, answered.** (`DESIGN.md` › *Colors*, `components.status-tag-live`, `components.table-row`.) Asked directly: **the `status-tag-live` border is not a 1.4.11 failure, but it is a design failure.** 1.4.11 requires 3:1 for visual information *required to identify* a component or state, and for graphical objects *required to understand* content. The tag's state is carried by the word "Live" at `#000000` on `#FFFFFF` (21:1), so the border is genuinely decorative and the tag is non-interactive (see M-2) — it is not a component boundary in the 1.4.11 sense, and I would not write it up as a violation. What it *is*: at 1.398:1 the outline is invisible to a low-vision or reduced-contrast user, so the spine's "three status tags, and only three" system renders as **two coloured blocks plus one run of ordinary text**, and the deliberate visual parallelism that makes `Live`/`Offline`/`Smart TV` read as one scale is lost precisely for the users who most need the scale. The same 1.398:1 value doing duty as the sole boundary between adjacent interactive rows *is* a finding, and it is filed as H-4.
  *Fix:* raise `divider` to a value that clears 3:1 on white (`#949494` is 3.03:1; `#8A8A8A` is 3.35:1 with margin) and accept the slightly heavier table, or give `status-tag-live` a `#666666` border (5.742:1) and keep `#DADADA` for row dividers only. The second is cheaper and preserves the hairline register.

- **M-2 — The spine never states that status tags are non-interactive, so its own 44px claim is unverifiable.** (`DESIGN.md` › `components.status-tag-*` (`padding: 4px 7px`, `typography.label` 10px/1); `EXPERIENCE.md` › *Accessibility Floor* › "Targets ≥44px on every interactive element".) A tag computes to roughly 20px tall — well under both 44px and the actual AA threshold. It is *inferable* from "Rows are not selectable; the whole row is one link" that tags are decorative children of the row link, and if so no target-size criterion applies and the spine is fine. But it is never said, and an implementer adding a filter-by-status affordance to a tag creates a 20px target without contradicting anything written. Two citation notes while here: **2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum) is AA at 24×24 CSS px**; 44×44 is **2.5.5 (Enhanced), AAA** — the spine's 44px floor is *stricter* than its stated target, which is good, but the loose citation invites someone to "correct" it downward. And **Focus Appearance is 2.4.13 (AAA)** in the published WCAG 2.2; **2.4.11 is Focus Not Obscured (Minimum), AA**. The focus failures in C-1 bite through 1.4.11 and 2.4.7, not through Focus Appearance — which matters, because it makes them mandatory rather than aspirational.
  *Fix:* add one line to *Component Patterns*: "Status tags are non-interactive text-and-fill indicators. They are never buttons, never filters, and never independently focusable." Then the 44px claim is true as stated.

- **M-3 — The 2px focus offset has nowhere to go in a zero-gap, full-bleed system, and 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured is never considered.** (`DESIGN.md` › `components.focus-ring`, *Layout & Spacing*.) `{spacing.section-gap}` is 0, regions abut on 1px rules, and below 640px "regions run full-bleed to the viewport edge". A ring drawn 2px *outside* its element therefore lands on the neighbouring region's ground, on a 1px structural rule (`#0F2BFF` on `#000000` = **2.810:1**), on a signal fill (C-1), or outside the viewport where it is clipped. Separately, the spine never states whether the alarm banner is sticky. If it is — and "the banner is the first thing on the page" plus its role as the only channel both push that way — then any focused element scrolled beneath it fails **2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum), AA**, which is the real AA criterion in this neighbourhood.
  *Fix:* state that the banner is static-flow, not sticky (or, if sticky, mandate `scroll-padding-block-start` equal to its height on the scroll container — a one-line fix that is trivial to lose). Add a rule that the focus ring may use `outline-offset: 0` with a 2px inner white/black companion where an element is flush to a region boundary, so the indicator never depends on space the system does not have.

- **M-4 — "Tab order equals visual order via DOM order" is asserted without forbidding the three things that break it, and the reflow breakpoint is where it will break.** (`EXPERIENCE.md` › *Bilingual Behaviour* › Mirroring; *Accessibility Floor*.) Ruling out a hand-maintained `tabindex` map is necessary but nowhere near sufficient. DOM order diverges from visual order under `order`, `flex-direction: row-reverse`, `grid-template-areas`, explicit `grid-row`/`grid-column` placement, and `position` games — none of which the spine prohibits, in either app. The concrete risk is the 640–1023px rule, "table rows retain their grid but **drop the secondary columns into a second line**": achieving that by explicit grid placement rather than by source order is the obvious implementation, and it silently reorders `last-confirmed time` and `status tag` relative to the DOM. In RTL this is doubly invisible to an LTR-reading reviewer. 1.3.2 Meaningful Sequence (A) and 2.4.3 Focus Order (A). Two smaller LTR assumptions in the same area: the ICU bidi-isolation contract (M-7) and the fact that `mockups/` — the artefact where mirrored order would actually be checkable — does not exist, leaving `.working/direction-signal.html` as the only RTL rendering, and that file uses `inset-inline-end` for the trial badge, i.e. the exact property DESIGN.md bans for the player.
  *Fix:* convert the outcome into a mechanical prohibition in the same style as FR-47's: "no `order`, no `row-reverse`/`column-reverse`, no `grid-template-areas`, and no explicit grid line placement in any component that contains more than one focusable element — reflow is achieved by source order and auto-placement only." That is testable by lint, which the current phrasing is not.

- **M-5 — The trial badge is the spine's only translucency, contradicts its own absolute rule, and computes to 2.12:1.** (`DESIGN.md` › `components.trial-badge` (`opacity: 0.3`), *Elevation & Depth*.) *Elevation & Depth* states "no translucency. **This is absolute and has no documented exception**" — and the component block sets `opacity: 0.3` two hundred lines later. On the owner register that resolves to `#B2B2B2` on white, **2.12:1**; on a dark ground, `#545454`, **2.614:1**. Whether this is a 1.4.3 failure turns on one question the spine does not answer: the mock renders the badge as the wordmark "Lawha", which is exempt as a **logotype**; but FR-79–82 call it a *trial* badge, and if it conveys trial/plan status it is informational text at 2.12:1, at ≤2% of screen area, viewed at 2–4 metres. Also note that `maxAreaRatio: 0.02` is functionally a fixed-dimension text container, which the spine forbids elsewhere (FR-48) — the badge cannot reflow when the Arabic string runs 30% longer.
  *Fix:* decide and record which it is. If it is the wordmark, say "wordmark only, no plan or trial wording" and the contrast question closes on the logotype exemption. If it must convey trial state, it is content: drop the opacity to 1.0 with `{colors.muted-foreground}` (5.742:1) and shrink instead, and reconcile the resulting contradiction in *Elevation & Depth* explicitly rather than leaving two absolute statements in conflict.

- **M-6 — The customer-register holding card's only visible object is invisible, which defeats the goal it exists to serve.** (`DESIGN.md` › `components.holding-card-customer`; `.working/direction-signal.html` › `.tvc .mark`.) The card is `#0A0A0A` ground, "an unbranded 32×32 mark, no text of any kind", and the mark is a 1px `{colors.divider-dark}` `#2A2A2A` outline — **1.379:1**, at 32px, on a 1920px-wide panel, at 2–4 metres. On a real TV with any ambient light it is not there. The stated purpose is that the stopped-subscription state "must be non-embarrassing on a public wall" — but a near-black screen with no discernible content reads to a venue's customers as a **broken or dead TV**, which is the specific embarrassment the two-register decision was made to avoid. Under 1.4.11 the mark is a graphical object required to understand the content (it is the *only* content), so 3:1 applies; more importantly, the product intent fails before the criterion does.
  *Fix:* raise the mark to a fill or border that clears 3:1 on `#0A0A0A` (`#4A4A4A` is 3.03:1; `#555555` is 3.61:1) and size it in `vmin` rather than px so it survives 720p and 4K. It stays anonymous and unbranded either way — "quiet" and "absent" are not the same requirement.

- **M-7 — 3.3.1/3.3.3 are handled beautifully in copy and not at all in the accessibility tree; and the bidi contract may inject control characters into announcements.** (`EXPERIENCE.md` › *Voice and Tone*, *Component Patterns* › Pairing code input / Media uploader; *Bilingual Behaviour* › Numerals.) The error *content* is the best part of this spine — wrong code vs expired code vs entitlement ceiling, format vs size vs codec, each naming condition and next action (NFR-9). What is missing is the wiring: no `aria-invalid`, no `aria-describedby` association between a field and its message, no statement of where focus goes on a failed submit, and no live-region channel for the uploader's per-file rejections (H-1). 3.3.1 is satisfied by text being present, but only reliably reaches AT through association — and 1.3.1/4.1.2 require it. Separately, on the bidi question: "isolation is applied once at the ICU placeholder boundary" (AD-22) is the right architecture, and logical DOM order means a screen reader receives the Arabic sentence and its LTR timestamp in correct reading order — so the aria-live announcement works. The risk is *how* isolation is implemented: the standard ICU approach inserts Unicode controls (U+2066 LRI / U+2069 PDI / U+061C ALM), and those characters landing inside an `aria-live` region's text node are a known source of stray or dropped announcements in some screen readers and of literal garbage on refreshable braille displays. The spine also never sets `aria-atomic`, so a partial update to the region can announce a fragment stripped of its isolation context.
  *Fix:* specify that isolation at the placeholder boundary is realised as **markup** (`<span dir="ltr">` / `<bdi>`) in DOM-rendered strings and reserved for control characters only where markup is impossible; set `aria-atomic="true"` on the status region; and add a line to the Accessibility Floor requiring every error message to be programmatically associated with its control (`aria-describedby` + `aria-invalid`) with focus moved to the first invalid control on submit.

### Low (6)

- **L-1 — Two numeric slips, both conservative.** `#FFFFFF` on `#0A0A0A` is **19.798:1**, stated as "~19.7:1"; `#0F2BFF` on `#0A0A0A` is **2.649:1**, stated as "2.5:1". Neither changes a decision. *Fix:* 19.8:1 and 2.65:1.

- **L-2 — The memlog contradicts the spine on electric-as-text, and the spine is right.** `.memlog.md` line 41 records "amber #FFB800 **and electric #0F2BFF** cannot serve as text colour on white at AA — restricted to solid fills". Electric is **7.474:1** and passes comfortably at every size; DESIGN.md and EXPERIENCE.md both corrected this. *Fix:* nothing structural, but the stale decision record is the kind of thing that gets re-litigated in implementation — append a correction line.

- **L-3 — The player's reduced-motion honour is technically correct and practically unreachable, and 2.2.2 relies on an undocumented exception.** `prefers-reduced-motion` degrading the 200ms cross-fade to a hard cut is exactly right (and **2.3.3 Animation from Interactions is AAA and does not apply anyway** — auto-advance is not interaction-triggered, so the cross-fade raises no AA issue). But the player takes no locale and no input from its device, runs on a Tizen TV whose owner cannot set an OS motion preference, and serves an audience of passers-by who certainly cannot. Meanwhile playlist auto-advance is auto-updating content lasting well over 5 seconds with no pause control — **2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide is Level A**, and conformance here rests entirely on the "essential" exception, which is defensible for digital signage but is never written down. *Fix:* carry a `reducedMotion` flag in the manifest so the console can set it per screen (the player already "takes language and direction from the manifest" — this is the same mechanism, and it makes the preference actually reachable); and document the 2.2.2 essential-exception claim in the Accessibility Floor so it is a decision rather than an omission.

- **L-4 — 1.4.12 Text Spacing sets up an unresolvable tension with the Arabic zero-tracking rule.** The spine forbids `letter-spacing` on Arabic at every tier, correctly, because tracking breaks cursive joins. 1.4.12 (AA) requires content to remain functional when a *user* applies `letter-spacing: 0.12em`, and grants no Arabic exception. A user stylesheet will break Arabic joins and there is nothing the spine can do about it. The genuinely good news is that "no fixed width or height on any text container" (FR-48) means the 1.4.12 *clipping* requirement — the part that actually fails in practice — is already satisfied by construction, including the `label` tier's `lineHeight: 1` and `typography.code`'s. *Fix:* note the tension so nobody "fixes" it by clamping Arabic containers; verify `button-primary.minBlockSize`, `nav-item.minBlockSize` and `table-row.minBlockSize` are implemented as `min-height`, never `height`.

- **L-5 — `mockups/` does not exist, and the only rendered reference contradicts the spine in five ways.** Both spines cite `mockups/` as holding "the promoted key screens"; the directory is absent. The surviving reference, `.working/direction-signal.html`, uses white text on `#FF2D00` (3.732:1, the combination DESIGN.md names as failing), `ui-monospace` for the code and timestamps (the spine states "there is no monospace family in this system"), `Geeza Pro`/`Noto Naskh Arabic` rather than Cairo, `text-transform: uppercase` carrying the label tier (the spine abandons uppercase as the mechanism), and `inset-inline-end` on the trial badge (banned for the player). The spines say they win on conflict, so this is not a governance failure — but it is the only artefact an implementer can *see*, and every one of its deviations is in an accessibility-relevant direction. *Fix:* either produce `mockups/` with the corrected tokens or remove the reference and mark the direction file "superseded — palette only".

- **L-6 — "Live" / "Offline" / "Smart TV" as accessible names.** Asked directly: `Live` and `Offline` are sufficient — they are unambiguous state words and, folded into the row's single accessible name alongside the screen name and time, they read correctly. **`Smart TV` is not.** It names a *device category*, not the state it encodes, which per the Truth Contract is "Lawha cannot restart this screen after a power cut" — a limitation that has nothing to do with being a smart TV as far as the user's mental model goes, and that reads as a neutral hardware fact rather than a caveat. A screen-reader user hearing "Drive-Thru Menu, Lunch Menu, now, Smart TV" gets no signal that anything is qualified; the amber fill that carries the caveat visually is exactly the channel AT does not receive. *Fix:* keep `Smart TV` as the visible label if the plain-language register demands it, but give the tag an `aria-label` (one ICU entry) that names the condition — "Limited recovery" or "Cannot restart after power cut" — and rely on the in-row sentence for the rest. This is the one place in the product where colour *is* carrying meaning that the text label does not.

## What the spine gets right

Credit where it is due, because it is substantial and it is why the findings above are worth acting on rather than rewriting from scratch:

- **The contrast arithmetic is honest.** Seven of nine stated ratios are exact to the published decimal and the two misses understate the true value. Somebody actually computed these. In particular the spine independently found that **white on `#FF2D00` fails at 3.732:1** and chose black at 5.627:1 — the correct and slightly counter-aesthetic call — and correctly confined amber to fill-only at 1.734:1 while permitting electric as text at 7.474:1.
- **Dark mode was reasoned, not recoloured.** Identifying that `#0F2BFF` collapses on near-black and introducing `electric-dark` is exactly the right move, and `#0A0A0A` over `#000000` so a true-black holding card still reads as a distinct surface is a genuinely thoughtful player-specific decision.
- **The core premise survives 1.4.1.** "Health is the absence of signal" *could* have been a textbook use-of-colour failure. It is not, because every status tag carries a text label as well as a fill and the spine says so explicitly and repeatedly. The `#FF2D00`/`#FFB800` pair also separates by luminance (3.732 vs 1.734 against white), so it survives protanopia and deuteranopia too. The premise's real weakness is the loading-state hole (H-2), not the colour question.
- **2.5.7 Dragging Movements (new in 2.2) is satisfied deliberately and by name** — "Reorder is available by keyboard and by explicit move controls, **never drag-only**". Most spines miss this criterion entirely.
- **The disclosure-is-in-the-DOM rule, with its reason stated.** "Escalated rows expand in place — never a tooltip, never a hover, never a modal, because the disclosure has to survive a touch device and a screen reader", reinforced by "this is the accessibility reason the disclosure may never become a tooltip". That is a designer reasoning from the accessibility tree outward, and it is the single best line in either document.
- **No fixed-dimension text containers (FR-48)**, which pre-satisfies the hard part of 1.4.12 and most of 1.4.10 for free, and is stated as an absolute rather than a preference.
- **`lang` and `dir` on the root plus `lang` on embedded runs of the other language**, with the correct reason (voice switching) — 3.1.1 and 3.1.2 handled.
- **Self-hosted, subset, precached faces so the Arabic display face never falls back**, with the failure mode named ("a player that loses its Arabic face when the network drops fails the Arabic test").
- **`polite`, not `assertive`, with the reasoning shown** — "this is not an interruption-worthy event, it is a status change". Correct, and rarer than it should be.
- **A 44px target floor that exceeds the AA requirement** (2.5.8 asks 24px), applied at every breakpoint including the 390px pairing case.
- **The alerting risk is disclosed rather than buried.** *The Truth Contract* names the dashboard-only limitation, attributes the decision, and states the consequence — which is what let this review find C-1 quickly, because the spine had already identified the alarm banner as the load-bearing object.

The distance from here to a defensible AA claim is three Critical fixes and a set of specification lines, not a redesign. The premise holds; the boundaries it creates were never measured.
