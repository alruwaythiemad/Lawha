Lawha — UX Design Validation Report

_bmad-output/planning-artifacts/ux-designs/ux-the_project-2026-08-11/{DESIGN,EXPERIENCE}.md

Two lenses ran in parallel over the spine pair: the rubric walker (43 findings — 2 critical, 8 high, 18 medium, 15 low) and an accessibility lens (22 findings — 3 critical, 6 high, 7 medium, 6 low). They converged: the direction is genuinely committed, every {path.to.token} reference resolved in both directions, all three load-bearing Chromium 76 capability claims checked out, and the contrast arithmetic was verified honest — seven of nine stated ratios exact to the published decimal, the two misses conservative.

Neither lens found the premise wrong. Both found the same structural weakness from different angles: every failing combination in this system lives at a boundary the spine never measured, and all of them are boundaries its own zero-gap, full-bleed, solid-fill grammar creates. The accessibility lens put it sharpest — the product's single most important object, the non-dismissible alarm banner, had no conformant treatment for a link or a focus ring on its red ground, worst case 1.03:1 in dark mode, which is not merely non-conformant but not rendered to the human eye.

All 5 criticals and all 14 highs have been applied to the spines. Three items could not be closed in UX and are recorded as Open Items with owners and gates: the shadcn/Radix stack claim (architecture's call), the Clerk auth audit (a launch gate, not a phase-3 revisit), and flash safety on owner-uploaded video (WCAG 2.3.1, Level A, with a physical-harm dimension and a v1 scope cost). Findings marked open below are those three plus deliberate non-changes.

2. Token completeness

thin

All references resolved; nothing dangling in either direction. Every stated contrast ratio recomputed from sRGB relative luminance and found correct. The failures were tokens that did not exist for cases the prose committed to.

criticalfixed

The Arabic type ramp was incomplete — Arabic headings would have rendered in a Latin family

DESIGN.md § typography
heading-ar and body-sm-ar did not exist, while heading and body-sm hardcoded Inter — which has no Arabic coverage. nav-item and both holding cards resolve through body-sm, so an Arabic nav label would fall back to a host font: the exact failure FR-51/FR-52 and AD-23 exist to prevent, and precisely what success criterion 3 is judged on ("no fallback font").
Fix: Both tiers added, plus a normative rule — every Latin tier has an -ar counterpart, and a Latin family never renders Arabic.
criticalfixed

There was no player type scale, and the two spines contradicted each other on player sizing

DESIGN.md § typography / EXPERIENCE.md § Responsive
EXPERIENCE.md committed to vmin sizing; DESIGN.md contained zero vmin values and every tier in px. A 56px pairing code is 5.2% of viewport height at 1080p and 2.6% at 4K, where it is unreadable across a café. The player's holding cards, instructions and badge had no size tokens at all.
Fix: Added player-code, player-headline, player-body, player-label and their -ar counterparts in vmin, a separate code-console tier for the console input, and a normative unit rule.
highfixed

The focus ring was unusable on the product's loudest component

DESIGN.md § components.focus-ring, banner-alarm
electric on alarm is 2.00:1; electric-dark on alarm is 1.03:1. The spine had also forbidden both obvious escapes ("never suppressed, never replaced by a background change"), and the banner's link colour was unspecified. Root cause: the rule that alarm and amber are theme-invariant colliding with electric being the one token that must shift.
Fix: Added on-signal / on-signal-companion and a focus-ring-on-signal double ring at outline-offset: 0 — the pair always clears 3:1 against any ground, which neither colour does alone. Links on signal fills are underlined at 2px, the one place this system accepts an underline.
highfixed

The -dark suffix carried two incompatible meanings, and a naive resolver would invert a load-bearing decision

DESIGN.md § components.holding-card-customer
In colors, -dark means "the dark-theme value". In the customer holding card it meant "literally near-black on a player that has no theme". Any consumer applying the obvious substitution either leaves the card unchanged by accident or flips it to white — on a public wall.
Fix: Introduced player-neutral-ground and player-neutral-mark as explicit non-theme tokens, and reserved -dark strictly for theme pairs.
highfixed

No component token declared its dark-mode resolution, so "both themes" was unverifiable from the artefact

DESIGN.md § components.*
All 17 component objects referenced light tokens only. An implementer reading the frontmatter literally ships #000000 text on #0A0A0A. Four dark tokens were referenced by nothing at all.
Fix: Added a normative theme-substitution rule with its three theme-invariant exceptions enumerated — which is what surfaces the signal-surface problem above rather than hiding it.
mediumfixed

The divider at 1.40:1 was the sole boundary between adjacent interactive rows, violating the spine's own rule

DESIGN.md § Colors, components.screen-row
The spine states divider is "never the only thing distinguishing two interactive targets" — and then the console's primary unit, on the home surface, did exactly that, with the whole row as one link.
Fix: The row's divider promotes to full-strength border on hover and focus-within — an inversion-consistent affordance in a system with no motion or radius. The status-tag-live border moved to muted-foreground (5.74:1) so the three-tag scale does not collapse for a low-vision user.
mediumfixed

The trial badge had no colour, and contradicted the spine's own absolute on translucency

DESIGN.md § components.trial-badge, Elevation & Depth
Elevation & Depth said "no translucency — absolute and no documented exception", and the component set opacity: 0.3 two hundred lines later. It also had no foreground while compositing over arbitrary customer content, and maxAreaRatio is functionally a fixed-dimension text container.
Fix: Declared wordmark only — never plan or trial wording — so the logotype exemption applies and it carries no state; trial status is stated in the console in words. White at 0.3. The single translucency exception is now enumerated in Elevation & Depth rather than left as a contradiction.
lowfixed

Two numeric slips and one over-broad absolute

DESIGN.md § Colors, Typography
electric on near-black stated 2.5:1, actually 2.65:1. Dark-mode ink stated 21:1, actually 19.8:1. The label tier's "and by nothing else" was contradicted by five components that name their own foreground. {spacing.rule} was defined and referenced nowhere.
Fix: All corrected; {spacing.rule} now referenced by every component border.

EXPERIENCE.md § State Patterns
The player's offline story was excellent. Accessibility review

review-accessibility.md

Verdict: not shippable against WCAG 2.2 AA as written — but the failures are concentrated, not systemic. The palette arithmetic is trustworthy and the premise survives scrutiny: "health is the absence of signal" does not fail 1.4.1, because every status tag carries a text label, and the alarm/amber pair separates by luminance so it survives protanopia and deuteranopia too. Roughly two-thirds of findings were specification gaps rather than design dead-ends — the spine repeatedly stated an accessible outcome without specifying the mechanism that makes it true or the thing that must be forbidden to keep it true.

The lens also corrected two of my own citations, both load-bearing: Focus Appearance is 2.4.13 (AAA) in published WCAG 2.2 while 2.4.11 is Focus Not Obscured (AA) — so the focus failures bite through 1.4.11 and 2.4.7 and are mandatory, not aspirational; and 2.5.8 asks 24px, so the spine's 44px floor is stricter than required rather than barely meeting it.

criticalfixed

C-1 — The alarm banner is the product's only alerting channel and was the least accessible object in the system

DESIGN.md § banner-alarm, focus-ring
Every available treatment failed. An electric link is 2.00:1 on red. A black link passes contrast but is visually identical to the surrounding banner text — no underline, no colour, no weight — failing 1.4.1 by having no carrier at all, which is worse than the failure mode 1.4.1 was written for. The focus ring is 2.00:1 light and 1.03:1 dark, where it is not rendered to the human eye. And with section-gap: 0 plus a 2px offset, a ring on any element merely abutting the banner bleeds onto red — guaranteed, not incidental.
Fix: on-signal double ring at zero offset; links on signal fills underlined at 2px; the banner declared static-flow rather than sticky.
criticalopen — product owner

C-2 — The AA commitment was made on a surface the spine declines to specify, with the audit deferred past the Arabic launch

EXPERIENCE.md § Bilingual Behaviour
Clerk hosted auth is step 1 of the only validated journey and mandatory for 100% of users. Conformance is not delegable — under EN 301 549 the service provider answers for embedded third-party components, and a WCAG claim covers complete processes. Unverified at the front door: lang/dir on the hosted page (if Clerk does not emit lang="ar" dir="rtl", an Arabic screen-reader user gets Arabic spoken by a Latin voice engine — total failure, not degradation), contrast against Clerk's tinted greys, its focus ring, whether its email-code flow permits paste, reflow at 320px. As written, Arabic ships non-conformant at its entry point by plan.
Fix: The AA claim is now scoped to the two apps with authentication explicitly unverified, and the audit is recorded as an Open Item recommending a launch gate rather than a phase-3 revisit — because the outcome decides whether headless components are a phase-3 task or a phase-1 architectural commitment.
criticalopen — product owner

C-3 — Owner-uploaded video plays full-screen on a public wall with no flash-safety gate

EXPERIENCE.md § Media uploader
The uploader validates format, size and codec only. 2.3.1 Three Flashes or Below Threshold is Level A and applies to user-generated content the service delivers. A café owner uploading a supplier's promo reel with a strobe cut can trigger a photosensitive seizure in a passer-by who never chose to look at the screen and cannot leave its field of view. The one finding here with a physical-harm and product-liability dimension rather than a usability one — and neither spine, the discovery log, nor any mock mentioned flashing anywhere.
Fix: Recorded as an Open Item requiring a v1 scope decision — at minimum a stated content policy plus warning-and-acknowledgement at upload for video; ideally luminance-flash analysis on ingest with rejection copy in the existing shape. Not silently adopted, because it changes scope.
highfixed

H-1 — The product had one live region and needed at least seven

EXPERIENCE.md § Accessibility Floor
Every other dynamic status was silent to assistive technology: per-file upload progress and rejection, optimistic rollbacks the user did not initiate, the no-Publish-button propagation status (the flagship FR-37 moment), at-limit blocks, the schedule collision sentence, the bulk-assignment confirmation and its undo, and the partial-load statement. Each is a 4.1.3 failure.
Fix: One global role="status" announcer with aria-atomic and a documented politeness contract; role="alert" reserved for rollback alone; and a rule that every state in the matrix names its announcement channel.
highfixed

H-2 — "No signal" was indistinguishable from "not yet loaded", on a product whose thesis is that silence means verified-healthy

EXPERIENCE.md § State Patterns
Sighted users were fine. But with skeletons textless, nothing animating, and no aria-busy anywhere, the three quiet states — loaded-and-healthy, still-loading, loaded-but-partial — collapsed into the same silence for a screen-reader user. The premise is only safe if absence is reliably distinguishable from absence of data, and it was not.
Fix: aria-busy on loading regions with a polite loaded/loading pair, and the row's accessible name now asserts health — "Live, confirmed just now" — converting an absence into a statement at zero visual cost. Visual health stays silent; the accessibility tree does not.
highpartly open — product owner

H-3 — The pairing code is a cognitive-function test with paste explicitly forbidden and an unspecified alphabet

EXPERIENCE.md § Pairing code input
Device pairing authenticates a player into the workspace, so 3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (AA) applies. The usual escape hatch for one-time codes is that they can be pasted — closed here by design decision, with no alternative path, since this is "the only path a screen enters the workspace". Nothing excluded 0/O, 1/I/l, 5/S, 2/Z, 8/B, and Inter's zero is undotted. Six glyphs at 56px also overflow both the spine's own 390px constraint and 1.4.10's 320px.
Fix applied: alphabet restricted to an unambiguous set and stated next to the token; the code tier split so the console input has its own smaller size. Open: the non-transcription alternative — a QR on the player deep-linking a pre-filled pairing URL — removes the cognitive test entirely and shortens Yusuf's path, but it is a new player surface and a scope addition.
highfixed

H-4 — The screen row had no interactive affordance and an accessible name that collided with the no-concatenation rule

EXPERIENCE.md § Screen row
Row text is black and grey, not electric; no underline; radius, shadow and transition are all zero so there is no hover or press affordance to fall back on; and the only boundary between one 48px link and the next was a 1.398:1 divider — violating DESIGN.md's own rule. Separately, folding four columns into "a single accessible name" is by definition the sentence assembly AD-22 forbids, and the spine did not say which rule bends.
Fix: Divider promotes to full-strength border on hover and focus-within; the accessible name is specified as one ICU entry with four named slots, so both rules hold.
highfixed

H-5 — No bypass mechanism, on a product where two persistent blocks precede every page's content

EXPERIENCE.md § Accessibility Floor
A keyboard user meets a persistent nav rail and, whenever any screen is offline, a full-bleed non-dismissible banner containing a link, positioned first. No skip link, no landmarks, no roles specified anywhere. 2.4.1 is Level A. Note the lens also cleared the accepted risk: 2.2.4 Interruptions is AAA, so non-dismissibility is not an AA failure and the product owner's decision stands.
Fix: Skip-to-content link plus a landmark map, and the banner given role="region" with an accessible name so it can be navigated to deliberately as well as past.
mediumfixed

M-3 / M-4 / M-6 / M-7 — Four specification gaps behind stated outcomes

multiple
The 2px focus offset had nowhere to go in a zero-gap full-bleed system, and banner stickiness was never stated (2.4.11). "Tab order via DOM order" forbade a tabindex map but not order, row-reverse, grid-template-areas, or explicit grid placement — and the 640–1023px column-wrap rule is exactly where the obvious implementation breaks it. The customer holding card's only visible object was 1.379:1, reading as a dead television — the precise embarrassment the two-register decision exists to avoid. And the ICU bidi contract, if realised with Unicode control characters, injects them into live-region text.
Fix: Zero-offset double ring at boundaries and banner declared static; a lintable structural prohibition replacing the asserted outcome; the mark raised to 3.61:1 and sized in vmin; isolation specified as markup with aria-atomic on the announcer.
lowfixed

L-3 / L-4 / L-6 — Three unreachable or unstated guarantees

multiple
Honouring prefers-reduced-motion was correct and practically unreachable: the player takes nothing from its device, and a Tizen TV's audience of passers-by can set no OS preference. Meanwhile playlist auto-advance is auto-updating content over 5 seconds with no pause control — 2.2.2 is Level A — resting entirely on the "essential" exception, which is defensible for signage but was never written down. And 1.4.12 grants no Arabic exception to the zero-tracking rule, a tension nobody should "fix" by clamping Arabic containers.
Fix: A reducedMotion flag carried in the manifest and set per screen from the console — the same channel as locale, and the only reachable one; the 2.2.2 essential-exception claim recorded as a decision; the 1.4.12 tension noted with the min-block-size rule that satisfies its clipping half.

Mechanical notes

  • All three Chromium 76 capability claims verified correct. inset-inline-* is Chrome 87 and unavailable; justify-items/align-items flow-relative values are Chrome 57 and available — and align-items being block-axis is desirable for a corner badge, since bottom stays bottom while the inline edge mirrors. clamp(), flexbox gap, aspect-ratio, :has(), container queries, oklch, color-mix all unavailable. vmin and compositor-driven opacity transitions available.
  • A fourth instance of the same bug class, caught by the rubric: the logical border shorthands (border-inline-start, border-block-end) shipped in Chrome 87 — the same batch as the insets the spine correctly flagged. Longhands are Chrome 69 and safe. Fixed: component tokens moved to longhand form, shorthands added to the ban list. The spine's precision in banning flexbox gap specifically is also correct and should not be "simplified" later — grid gap is Chrome 66 and the badge grid needs it.
  • The discovery log contained a wrong decision that both spines had silently corrected: a record that electric #0F2BFF cannot serve as AA text on white. It is 7.474:1 and passes at every size. A correction entry has been appended, since a stale decision record is what gets re-litigated during implementation.
  • Both files parse as valid YAML and carry every frontmatter key their specs name. DESIGN.md's description was three sentences where the spec says one line — shortened.
  • Zero unresolved {path.to.token} references across both files, before and after the fixes. Fourteen orphan tokens at review time; the four -dark orphans were symptoms of the missing theme-substitution rule and are now load-bearing.
